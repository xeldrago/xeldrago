Forehead Edition

One person holds the phone facing outward on their forehead. Everyone else describes the word (without saying it) until they guess it. Tilt the phone down for correct, up to pass — or use the on-screen buttons. Pick 📚 Study to play with exam facts: friends see the term (plus optional category, hint and fact) and describe it; passed terms are listed with their facts at the end for revision.

20 Questions

Pick a category, hand the phone to the "keeper." They read the word secretly, then everyone else asks yes/no questions to figure it out. Pick 📚 Study to quiz yourselves on exam facts — the keeper sees the answer and the facts to answer yes/no correctly, and hints unlock for the guessers after questions 10 and 15.

Wavelength

One team sees a hidden target on a slider between two opposites ("cold ↔ hot"); they give a one-word clue and the rest guess where the dial lands.

Taboo

Guess the word from a teammate's clues — without them using five forbidden related words. Great with the same phone-timer format as Heads Up.

Fishbowl

Same word set, three rounds: describe it, act it out (charades), then say one word only — memory carries it home.

Most Likely To

Read a prompt ("most likely to become famous"), everyone points at once — funniest for a full 6-person circle, no setup needed.

Category Blurt

Shout out items in a category ("Rajinikanth movies") in 30 seconds without repeating — last one standing wins the round.

Game log

Rules, boards & sources

Shared engine rules (all boards): four pawns each; a 1 brings a pawn onto its start square; a 6 gives another roll; one pawn moves per roll; finish needs an exact roll; landing on a lone opposing pawn on an ordinary square sends it to its yard; passing never captures; star squares (every start square and one more per arm) are safe for everyone; two or more pawns of one colour on an ordinary square form a blockade that opponents cannot land on or pass (no blockades on safe squares); pawns in home lanes cannot be captured. Bonus rolls for captures / reaching home and the three-sixes penalty are options in setup (off by default).

Classic (2–4): traditional four-player layout; entry is on a 1 (a house rule — the traditional entry roll is a 6). The 56-step / exact-finish / safe-square rules follow the commonly published Traditional Games Federation of India style rules; the blockade rule is this game's custom default, and the TGFI document itself is not bundled here, so check it for official rulings. 2 players sit on opposite arms; 3 players use three arms.

Five-arm (5): a custom variant, not an official ruleset. Same arm design in five equal sectors, 65 shared squares.

Six-arm (6): six equal arms, 78 shared squares, inspired by commercial six-player boards. No manufacturer rule sheet was available offline, so it uses the shared engine rules above and is NOT verified against a specific manufacturer's instructions.

How the boards were calibrated: one generator builds every board from a single arm template (3 wide × 6 long; clockwise track = 6 squares out the left column, a cap square, 6 back along the right column; middle column = private 5-square home lane). The hub size is solved so neighbouring arms' inner corners sit √2 apart, which reproduces the classic board exactly (R=2, 52 squares). Start = right column, 2nd from the cap; turn-in = the cap square, 2 squares before start, so every player walks (13·N − 2) shared squares + 5 lane squares + home. Rules read only these tables, never pixel positions. The self-tests check the topology and the rules on all three boards.


        

      

    

    

      

        
Business

        
Buy places, collect rent, upgrade and bankrupt your friends — 2 to 8 players on one phone. Everyone starts with equal money outside the board and must roll a 1 to enter, then roll again to move.

        
        
        
        
4

Rules for both modes
          
Both modes: one die. A player who is not yet on the board needs a 1 to enter (at GO) and then rolls again to move. Passing or landing on GO pays ₹200. Land on an unowned place to buy it (no auctions — skip to leave it for later). Rent is paid to the owner; owning a full colour set doubles the base rent. Stations/airports pay ₹25/50/100/200 for 1–4 owned; utilities pay 4× the die (10× with both). With a full colour set you can upgrade places for the group's price (evenly): 4 houses, then a hotel, each raising the rent. Tax squares pay the bank. Short of cash? Upgrades and places are sold to the bank at half price automatically; if that is not enough you are bankrupt and your assets go to the player you owed (or back to the bank). Last player standing wins, or tap “End game” to crown the richest.

          
Bankroll mode: the six Chance/Chest squares become Mystery squares: pay the bank ₹50–200, receive ₹50–200, or move to a random place. Jail costs ₹150 to leave (or wait up to 3 turns, then it is paid for you). The free-parking corner becomes the Trading Port: land there to trade places with another player.

          
Full Monopoly mode: Chance and Community Chest cards (including Get Out of Jail Free), Free Parking is empty, jail costs ₹50 or a card, or roll a 6 to break out (after 3 turns the fine is paid). Trading is open on your turn any time.

          
Simplified on purpose: single die (no doubles), no auctions, no mortgages, one place per side in a trade. This is not an official Monopoly or Plato Bankroll ruleset.

Raja Rani Manthri Thirudan Police

        
3–8 players, one shared phone, endless rounds. Papers are shuffled and picked in secret, the Thirudan winks, the Police hunts — and every round lands on the scoreboard.

        
5

        

        

        
        
        
        
Characters & scoring
          
👑 Raja +1000 · 👸 Rani +900 · 🎩 Manthri +800 · 🃏 Joker +600 (6 players). 🥛 Milk Man +500 · 📰 Paper Man +400. 3 players: Raja, Police, Thirudan. 4: + Manthri. 5: + Rani. 6: + Joker. 7: + Milk Man. 8: + Paper Man.

          
Police (announced publicly when the hunt starts): names the Thirudan before everyone is winked out → +1100, otherwise −200.

          
Thirudan winks at players other than the Police; each winked player announces “I’ve been winked!”. Every wink is worth 1100 ÷ (players − 2) of his escape total. Escapes (wrong accusation, or everyone winked out): +1100. Caught: −200 plus the wink points already earned.

          
These point values are this game’s house rules, not a traditional ruleset.

Imposter 🕵️

        
3–12 players, one shared phone. Everyone sees the secret word — except the imposter, who only gets a hint. Give clues, bluff, vote in secret, and score points over as many rounds as you like.

        
5

        

        

        
        
        
        
        

        
📚 Study mode — play with exam facts and learn while you bluff

        
        
        
        
        
How it plays & scoring
          
1) Each player privately taps to see their card and hides it again. Crew see the category and the secret word; the imposter sees IMPOSTER plus a hint (a different clue about the word — never the category itself, e.g. word Camel · category Animals · hint Sand). 2) A starter is picked (tick “Imposter never gets to start” to keep the imposter from going first); go round giving one-word clues while the timer runs. 3) Everyone votes in secret by passing the phone. 4) Reveal and score.

          
Points: +100 for each crew member who votes for a real imposter · +300 for an imposter who is not voted out (a tie votes out nobody) · +150 for a caught imposter who then guesses the word. Forgot your role? A floating button during the timer and voting lets you check your own card privately.

          
Study mode: pick study words (SSC CGL facts from the study packs) instead of, or mixed with, party words. The crew see the term plus two facts on their card; the imposter sees only the category and a one-word hint. The result screen shows the facts so everyone learns something. 413 built-in party words across 16 categories, plus your own custom words, written for this game (including Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Indian food sets). The scoring is a house rule, not an official one.
